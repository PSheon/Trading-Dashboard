  requests). Hence:
  - Every local-clock check (fresh) compares Date.now with Date.now values at most ~1.1 s apart: it can only
    refuse with a wall-clock step (backwards: ahead_of_clock; forwards > ~3.9 s: older_than_bound). Measured
    drift here 0-1 ms; not excluded for run 2 (not recorded then).
  - freshProvider ahead_of_clock needs our clock >= 2 s BEHIND Hyperliquid's: ours runs 0.1 s ahead and

PROPOSED FIX (not implemented; production behaviour unchanged)
  1. Diagnostic first (no behaviour change): on live_account_evidence_expired, log the check's name and the
     ages (site, local or provider, now - at, the bound; no address, no amount) as the observer already does
     for live_account_inconsistent_balances, or carry a names-only `check` on the LiveBoundaryError. Stage and
     mainnet logs then attribute every refusal. Cost: one log line per refusal.
  2. If it is the provider state time (expected): keep the 5 s bound (a state more than 5 s old is
     genuinely stale evidence: loosening it, or a separate larger PROVIDER_STATE_MAX_AGE_MS, weakens what
     the order rests on and would also pass a stalled chain), and make the refusal retryable once with a

     is correct. A re-subscribe inside the same clock is cheaper but rarely helps: the same stall answers
     the same old time and the 5 s window is already partly used.
  3. If it is a wall-clock step: measure the local-to-local intervals (started, observedAt, receivedAt,
     completedAt) on a monotonic clock (performance.timeOrigin + performance.now) and keep Date.now only
